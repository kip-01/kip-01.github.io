Kujenga Amani Pamoja: youth-led peace proposals

Three applications to a call for youth-led peacebuilding initiatives under the Kujenga Amani Pamoja programme, each led by a community youth organisation in Kwale County in autumn 2024.

The problem

Young people aged 14 to 26 in Tsimba-Golini, Kundutsi and Diani are being drawn into juvenile gangs and rivalries between neighbourhood hangouts, known locally as maskani. Conflict analysis sessions pointed to peer pressure, drug abuse, weak parenting and a gap between the generations. Women carry a large share of the harm.

Three initiatives

  • Transforming the mindset of youth (Tsimba-Golini). Courtesy visits to state actors, five dialogue forums, tujuane straight talks between youth and elders with theatre skits, and podcasts and radio. 180 direct and 3,000 indirect beneficiaries, with 60% young women.
  • Kujenga Amani Msambweni (Diani). Dialogue in the maskani on problems and then solutions, an inter-maskani ideas competition, and links to devolved funds for the best ideas. 140 direct beneficiaries.
  • Maskani Safi Program (Kundutsi and Mazimalume). Peace dialogues in four maskani, peace training for school and madrasa teachers, links to opportunities, and a cultural day that creates 'Amani Ambassadors'. 60 young men, 40 young women, 12 teachers and 100 students.

Shared design choices

Each application starts from a conflict analysis, plans for women's participation, and is community-owned: meetings run in local venues, with local leaders, Kaya elders and religious leaders. They use coastal culture as a peace tool, through shared meals, Swahili music and skits, and they plan local radio and social media so the message travels beyond the room.

What I did

Supported the three community organisations to turn conflict analysis into fundable applications: problem statements, objectives, intervention logic, activity plans, beneficiary numbers, gender and conflict-sensitivity sections and communication plans.
