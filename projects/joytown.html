The school

Joytown Secondary is a public national school under The Salvation Army. It serves learners with physical impairments from across Kenya, including cerebral palsy, spina bifida, muscular dystrophy and osteogenesis imperfecta. Its motto is Najimudu, "I am able".

Infrastructure that people can use

Through the Mseto programme I coordinated accessible ablution blocks, pathways, kitchens and water harvesting at Joytown and at two other special-needs institutions. Recent partner support continues the work: a donated solar pump has cut pumping costs, and every walkway is now roofed and fitted with support rails, so learners on wheelchairs and crutches can move safely between classrooms and dormitories in the rains.

Results and needs, in the numbers

The school's 2025 mean score in the national exam rose from 2.026 to 2.505, and about a quarter of students qualify for university. The same report is candid about what stands in the way, and sets three investment priorities for partners:

  • Digital inclusion. 20 laptops with adaptive keyboards and mice, and 8 mounted projectors, so learners with fine-motor challenges can type rather than handwrite.
  • Hygiene and dignity. A commercial washing machine and sanitary towels, because many learners cannot wash their own clothes and bedding.
  • Security and capacity. A perimeter wall, a larger dining hall and new dormitories. In 2025 the school turned away 86 eligible students for lack of space.
