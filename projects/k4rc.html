Kenya4Resilience Consortium (K4RC)

Four years as Project Coordinator on a consortium project in Wamba, Samburu East: helping pastoralist households move into climate-smart agropastoralism while building peace between neighbouring communities.

The context

Most people in Wamba are pastoralists. Droughts, shrinking pasture and disputes over land and water put households under pressure, and conflict between neighbouring communities makes every shock worse. The project worked on livelihoods and peace together because each depends on the other.

The approach

  • From pastoralism to agropastoralism. About 400 farmers were trained and given starter inputs. Six groups, including women, youth and persons with disabilities, now grow maize, beans, carrots, groundnuts, sweet potatoes, cowpeas and capsicum.
  • Learning by doing. Farmer-to-farmer visits, on-site training and exchange visits to established farms in Maralal and Thika, with extension support from the sub-county agriculture department.
  • Kitchen gardens that spread. Vertical sack gardens and small tilled plots were replicated across Wamba well beyond the project's own participants.
  • Pests handled in the field. When caterpillars from maize fields hit vegetables, the team added integrated pest management training and crop protection.
  • A local hub. The Samburu K4R Hub networks local organisations and government, with members from the Kenya Forest Service, schools, the ward administration and chiefs.

Peace results

Eighteen peacebuilding and cross-border dialogue forums brought together more than 450 people, including Samburu Morans and security agencies. Conflict incidents in the target areas fell by 45%, and 317 weapons were surrendered in voluntary disarmament in Wamba sub-county. Women and youth made up more than 60% of active participants and leaders.

What I did

  • Coordinated the consortium's field programme, partners and county links.
  • Set up agroecological demonstration plots, and trained grazing committees and conservancies in pasture regeneration and fodder banking.
  • Linked farmer groups to input suppliers, off-takers and local traders, improving market access for 800+ smallholders.
  • Led seven health outreaches with Wamba Level IV Hospital and AMREF, reaching about 300 households.
  • Tracked results with KoboCollect and Power BI, and wrote donor reports, outcome maps and 17 impact stories.
  • Produced more than 15 infographics and videos that the Tana River and Turkana chapters reused.
