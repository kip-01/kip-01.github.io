Resilient Nursery Schools Project

A three-year proposal to improve the health, nutrition and safety of children in 13 nursery schools across five counties of western Kenya, delivered mainly through mobile health outreach with county governments.

The problem

Kenya has more than 40,000 early childhood development centres. Many lack play and learning materials as well as health and nutrition services, and receive little government funding. In poor rural settlements, children also face weak nutrition, unsafe water and limited access to health care.

The design

A fully equipped mobile outreach team visits the schools with county health workers, backed by home visits through community health volunteers. Five outcomes structure the work:

  • Health and nutrition. Growth monitoring, deworming, vitamin A, immunisation checks and referrals, plus school feeding and food-safety training.
  • Capable parents and teachers. Child health, first aid and hazard assessments for classrooms, play areas and perimeters.
  • WASH in schools. Safe water, child-friendly sanitation, handwashing and hygiene training.
  • Child rights and protection. Birth certificates for every enrolled child and rights training for parents, teachers and children.
  • Monitoring and evaluation. A baseline survey, field visits, regular reports and an end-of-project evaluation.

A scoping decision

The first version targeted 50 schools. When I tested the planned outcomes against the duration and budget, wide coverage would not have produced tangible impact. The proposal therefore targets 13 schools, with scale-up to the remaining areas in a later phase if funding allows.

Cross-cutting themes

Gender (including men's role in child care), environment, HIV and AIDS, conflict and peace messages, and disability inclusion are built into every activity. Sustainability rests on county government taking over outreach, trained community health volunteers, and parents who can demand services.
