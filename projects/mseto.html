Mseto Resilience: WASH and Food Security

A resilience programme of The Salvation Army Kenya East Territory. I worked on it as WASH and Livelihoods Project Officer from 2018 to 2020, from savings groups to accessible water and sanitation in special-needs institutions.

The programme

Its goal: empowered and healthy women, men and children who are able to realize their rights and improve their well-being despite shocks, stressors and uncertainty. It combines water, sanitation and hygiene, food security, health and rights, using a Faith Based Facilitation process in which communities analyse their own issues and plan the response. It contributes to SDGs 2, 3, 5, 6, 13 and 17.

What I did

  • Coordinated WASH and livelihood work that reached more than 1,200 agropastoral households.
  • Formed and trained 20 village savings and loan associations, producer groups and community organisations.
  • Supported disability inclusion in special-needs institutions: accessible ablution blocks, pathways, kitchens and water harvesting at Joytown, Thika High School for the Blind and Variety Village. See Joytown.
  • Delivered child protection, safeguarding and health promotion training for teachers, caregivers and learners.
  • Managed a commercial demonstration farm showing drip irrigation, soil moisture conservation, integrated pest management and mixed crop and livestock systems.
  • Ran gender-responsive livelihood work in poultry, kitchen gardening, beekeeping and basketry, and built conflict sensitivity into shared grazing and water arrangements.

Evidence it works

The programme's own study, cited in the 2019-2023 proposal, found that in Kilome-Sulutani 72.5% of households now harvest rainwater from roofs, against 50% in areas the project had not reached.

The next phase

The 2019-2023 proposal scales the model to 64 communities and 64 schools with about 31,200 direct right-holders, and adds a research and demonstration centre at Thika to link communities to research and industry.
